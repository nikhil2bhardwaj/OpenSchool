Theory

Esterification: a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid to form an ester and water. It is a reversible, acid-catalysed nucleophilic acyl substitution.

R–COOH + R′–OH ⇌ R–COOR′ + H2O  (conc. H2SO4, reflux)

Example: CH3COOH + C2H5OH ⇌ CH3COOC2H5 (ethyl ethanoate, fruity smell) + H2O. Salicylic acid + methanol gives methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen). Yield rises with catalyst amount and reflux time, and falls when water is present (hydrolysis shifts equilibrium back).

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Add concentrated H2SO4 dropwise with cooling — it is corrosive and the mixing is exothermic.
  • Reflux gently with a water condenser; never heat a closed vessel.
  • Keep the apparatus dry — water hydrolyses the ester and lowers the yield.
  • Confirm the ester by its characteristic fruity smell; do not inhale vapours directly (waft).
